## ARTICLE II: CONSTITUTIONAL FOUNDATION

### 2.1 Governing Authority

This Company operates under the authority of the God of the Bible. Objective morality exists—established by God, not human opinion. The Constitutional Commitments outlined in Section 2.2 are absolute and unchangeable.

### 2.2 The Nine Constitutional Commitments

The Company is bound by the following immutable principles:

1. **Human Dignity** – Every person bears God's image with sacred worth from conception onward. No dehumanization, exploitation, or discrimination.  
     
2. **Truth & Honesty** – Radical transparency required. No deception tolerated. Open finances, clear terms, kept promises. No dark patterns, hidden fees, or selective enforcement.  
     
3. **Ownership & Stewardship** – Property rights (including intellectual property) must be respected. Platform fee capped at maximum 30% of transaction value. No IP theft, unfair revenue deprivation, or exploitation.  
     
4. **Justice for the Vulnerable** – Special protection for those who cannot defend themselves. Equal treatment for small/large creators, clear appeals processes. No predatory monetization or unequal enforcement.  
     
5. **Sabbath Rest** – Technology serves human flourishing, not addiction. Sustainable work, healthy boundaries. No exploitation of FOMO, compulsive behaviors, or endless engagement.  
     
6. **Creative Freedom Within Bounds** – Wide artistic freedom exists within moral and legal boundaries. Content policy enforces PG-13 to R-rated standards comparable to published fiction. Forbidden: explicit sexual content, sexual assault (all depictions), graphic torture, sexualizing minors, hate speech. Allowed: violence within story context (brief descriptions), implied sexuality (fade-to-black), dark themes handled respectfully, controversial ideas, diverse political viewpoints.  
     
7. **Love & Community** – Everything done with love, not cruelty. Corrective moderation, genuine community, opt-in design. No harassment, coercion, or hostile environments.  
     
8. **Humility & Service** – The Company exists to serve, not be served. Admit mistakes publicly, incorporate feedback, prioritize user needs. No defensiveness or treating users as problems.  
     
9. **Freedom Within Boundaries** – Minimal intervention; wide ideological freedom within moral guardrails. "I disagree" or "This offends me" does not constitute grounds for content removal. Protect diverse viewpoints, consistent rules, user agency.

### 2.3 Immutability

These Constitutional Commitments cannot be amended, modified, or removed. Any attempt to do so triggers the stewardship protection mechanisms in Article IV.

### 2.4 Constitutional Charter

The detailed applications, prohibitions, and theological foundations for these Constitutional Commitments are set forth in the "Open Eppic Constitutional Charter" (hereinafter "Charter"), which is incorporated into this Operating Agreement by reference. The Charter provides authoritative interpretation of these Commitments.

## ARTICLE IV: STEWARDSHIP OWNERSHIP STRUCTURE

### 4.1 Purpose and Intent

This Article establishes the stewardship ownership model that protects Constitutional Commitments through structural mechanisms rather than penalty clauses. The Open Eppic Purpose Trust holds a Golden Share (Class B unit) with veto authority, and Class A Members voluntarily grant a Call Option allowing the Trust to purchase their units at $0.01 per unit upon constitutional violations.

This structure is based on established precedent from Patagonia (2022), Ecosia (2018), and Tony's Chocolonely (2021), and has been upheld in Delaware and Kansas corporate law as valid contractual mechanism.

### 4.2 Open Eppic Purpose Trust

**4.2.1 Trust Establishment**

A Kansas Charitable Trust shall be established under the name "Open Eppic Purpose Trust" with the sole purpose of safeguarding the Constitutional Commitments of Open Eppic LLC.

**4.2.2 Trust Governance**

The Trust shall be governed by three Trustees:

(a) **Theological Representative** – Seminary-trained theologian or equivalent with expertise in Christian ethics and moral philosophy

(b) **Community Representative** – Elected by platform creator community once platform reaches 1,000 active users; appointed by Founder until that threshold

(c) **Legal Representative** – Attorney licensed to practice law in Kansas with expertise in corporate governance

**4.2.3 Trustee Terms and Decisions**

- Trustees serve 3-year terms, staggered to ensure continuity  
- Trustee decisions require 2-of-3 majority vote  
- Founder retains right to appoint initial Trustees until 1,000 active users  
- Trustee compensation capped at $10,000/year per Trustee (indexed to inflation)

**4.2.4 Trust Authority**

The Trust, as holder of the Golden Share, possesses veto authority over Constitutional Matters per Section 3.2.1. Additionally, the Trust may exercise the Call Option upon determination of constitutional violation per Section 4.4.

### 4.3 Call Option Grant

**4.3.1 Voluntary Grant**

All Class A Members, as a condition of membership, voluntarily and irrevocably grant to the Open Eppic Purpose Trust a Call Option to purchase their Class A membership units at $0.01 (one cent) per unit.

This Call Option:

- Is voluntarily granted (not a penalty or forfeiture)  
- Is enforceable under Kansas contract law  
- Constitutes a fixed-price sale agreement, not penalty clause  
- May be exercised by Trust upon determination of constitutional violation  
- Survives transfer of units (binds successors and assigns)

**4.3.2 Legal Mechanism**

This Call Option is a legally enforceable contractual right, not a penalty clause. Courts have upheld fixed-price sale agreements as valid contracts. The Trust is purchasing units at an agreed-upon price ($0.01 per unit), not imposing a penalty.

### 4.4 Violation Triggers

**4.4.1 Bright-Line Triggers (Automatic Veto/Call Option Available)**

The following violations trigger immediate Golden Share veto and make Call Option exercisable:

(a) Constitutional Commitments removed, amended, or modified in this Operating Agreement  
(b) User data sold to third parties for profit  
(c) Platform fee exceeds 30% of transaction value  
(d) Policy changes applied retroactively within grandfathering periods for existing creator works  
(e) Content sexualizing minors knowingly promoted or not removed within 24 hours of discovery

**4.4.2 Pattern-Based Triggers (Trustee Determination Required)**

The following patterns trigger Call Option availability subject to 2-of-3 Trustee vote:

(a) Three or more violations of the same Constitutional Commitment within 12 months  
(b) Intentional deceptive practices (vs. honest operational mistakes)  
(c) Systematic unequal enforcement of policies (vs. case-by-case contextual judgment)  
(d) Actions demonstrating intent to circumvent Constitutional Commitments  
(e) Systematic failure to maintain radical transparency (missed financial reports, hidden decisions)

**4.4.3 Determining Violations**

Minor violations, honest mistakes, or good-faith errors in judgment do NOT automatically trigger the Call Option. The Trust exercises discretion guided by Constitutional Principles, recognizing that:

- Humans make mistakes (grace is extended)  
- Context matters (judgment required)  
- Intent matters (honest error vs. deliberate circumvention)  
- Patterns matter more than isolated incidents

### 4.5 Call Option Exercise Process

**4.5.1 Notice of Violation**

Upon determination that a trigger has occurred, the Trust shall provide written notice to all Class A Members within 7 business days, specifying:

(a) Which trigger occurred  
(b) Evidence supporting determination  
(c) Whether violation is bright-line or pattern-based  
(d) Whether Trust intends to exercise Call Option or allow restoration  
(e) Timeline for restoration (if applicable)

**4.5.2 Restoration Period (Optional)**

The Trust MAY, at its sole discretion, grant the Company a restoration period of 30-90 days to cure the violation before exercising the Call Option. Restoration requires:

(a) Violation corrected and Constitutional Commitment restored  
(b) Written plan submitted to Trust preventing recurrence  
(c) Public acknowledgment on Constitutional Transparency Blog  
(d) Trust approval (2-of-3 vote) that restoration is sufficient

**4.5.3 Call Option Exercise**

If the Trust determines restoration is impossible or Company fails to restore within the granted period, the Trust may exercise the Call Option:

(a) Trust provides 14 days written notice of Call Option exercise  
(b) Trust purchases all Class A membership units at $0.01 per unit  
(c) Trust pays total purchase price within 30 days  
(d) Ownership transfers to Trust

**4.5.4 Post-Exercise Actions**

After exercising Call Option, the Trust may:

(a) **Restore Compliance:** Appoint new management, restore constitutional operations, re-issue units to new members committed to constitutional fidelity, OR

(b) **Dissolve Company:** Initiate dissolution per Article IX, distributing remaining assets per constitutional priorities

The Trust prioritizes mission fidelity over financial returns. Dissolution is preferable to compromised operations.

### 4.6 Appeals Process

**4.6.1 Class A Member Appeal**

Class A Members may appeal a violation determination:

(a) Written appeal submitted to Trust within 14 days of violation notice  
(b) Appeal must provide evidence contradicting violation determination  
(c) Trust reviews appeal within 30 days (2-of-3 vote required to overturn)  
(d) Trust decision is final

**4.6.2 User Community Input**

At 1,000+ active users, Trust may solicit input from platform creator community before exercising Call Option for pattern-based violations. Community input is advisory, not binding.

### 4.7 Economic Effect

The Call Option achieves the same economic effect as a penalty clause without legal vulnerability:

**If Company violates Constitutional Commitments:**

- Trust exercises Call Option at $0.01 per unit  
- Class A Members lose virtually all equity value  
- Company cannot raise capital (equity worthless)  
- Company cannot be acquired (nothing of value to purchase)  
- Economic incentive strongly favors constitutional fidelity

**Unlike penalty clause:**

- This is voluntary sale agreement, not penalty  
- Enforceable under Kansas contract law  
- Established precedent from steward-ownership models

## ARTICLE VI: REVENUE DISTRIBUTION

### 6.1 Revenue Split Model

Platform revenue from creator content sales is distributed as follows:

**CREATOR:** 40% of gross transaction value  
**ELEMENT POOL:** 40% of gross transaction value (split among element owners)  
**PLATFORM:** 20% of gross transaction value

**Constitutional Cap:** Platform fee may not exceed 30% of transaction value under any circumstances. Current 20% rate is operational decision; any increase requires Purpose Trust approval if exceeding 30%.

### 6.2 Element Pool Distribution

The 40% Element Pool is split into two sub-pools:

**SMALL IP BUCKET:** 10% of transaction value (25% of Element Pool)

- Shared equally among all Small IP elements used in Work  
- Small IP \= independently created elements not part of major franchises  
- If 5 Small IP elements used: each receives 2% of transaction value

**BIG IP BUCKET:** 30% of transaction value (75% of Element Pool)

- Shared proportionally among Big IP elements based on weighted shares  
- Big IP \= elements from established franchises  
- Weighting: 1-30 shares per Big IP element (based on franchise size)  
- Platform assigns Big IP share counts (subject to appeal)

### 6.3 Revenue Thresholds

**Work Threshold:** Works must earn $10/month to trigger Element Pool splits

- Below $10/month: Creator receives 80%, Platform receives 20%, Element owners receive $0  
- Rationale: Protects small element owners from $0.50 payouts on tiny transactions

**Element Threshold:** Elements must earn $1/month from Element Pool or earnings forfeit to Work creator

- Prevents dust payments of $0.05 per element  
- Forfeited amounts redistribute to Work creator (not Platform)

**Payout Threshold:** Creators must accumulate $25 before payout is processed

- Payouts occur NET-30 (within 30 days of month-end)  
- Payment methods: PayPal, direct bank transfer (ACH), or alternative methods at Platform discretion

### 6.4 Same-Creator Element Rule

Elements created by the same person as the Work creator do NOT trigger Element Pool splits.

**Example:**

- Author writes story using 3 of their own elements \+ 2 external elements  
- Split: Author 40%, External Elements 40% (2 elements split this), Platform 20%  
- Author's own 3 elements contribute no additional split (author already receiving creator's 40%)

**Rationale:** Prevents gaming system by registering trivial elements

### 6.5 Anti-Gaming Protections

**10+ Element Review:**  
If Work uses 10+ elements from creators other than Work creator, automatic review flags potential gaming. Manual review determines if elements legitimately used or gaming attempt.

**Pattern Detection:**  
AI monitoring flags suspicious patterns:

- Single creator's elements dominating across multiple works by same author  
- New accounts with many elements used immediately  
- Low text relevance scores for attached elements  
- Reciprocal arrangements (A uses B's elements, B uses A's elements)

**Consequences:**  
Gaming confirmed \= both parties banned, all earnings forfeited.

### 6.6 Grandfathering of Revenue Splits

Once a Work is published, its revenue split is locked indefinitely (grandfathered). Future changes to revenue distribution model do NOT apply to already-published works.

**Example:**

- Work published in 2025 under 40/40/20 split  
- Platform changes to 50/30/20 split in 2027  
- 2025 Work continues using 40/40/20 forever

**Rationale:** Protects creator investment, prevents retroactive harm (Constitutional Commitment \#2)

## ARTICLE VII: PAYMENT PROCESSING AND LIMITED AGENT STRUCTURE

### 7.1 Platform as Limited Payment Agent

The Company acts as a limited payment collection agent on behalf of Creators and Element Owners (collectively, "Payees").

**7.1.1 Deemed Payment**

Upon purchase of any Work by an End User:

(a) Payees are deemed paid immediately upon the Company's receipt of payment from the End User, regardless of when funds are actually transferred to Payee accounts.

(b) The Company holds all funds in trust for Payees during the settlement period (NET-30 from month-end), acting as agent and not as principal.

(c) The Company is authorized to deduct Platform Fees (currently 20%, maximum 30% per Constitutional Cap) from gross revenue before distributing to Payees.

**7.1.2 Legal Purpose**

This structure is designed to comply with state Money Transmitter laws by establishing the Company as a limited payment collection agent, not a Money Transmitter. The Company does not hold user funds as principal; it holds them in trust as agent for Payees who are deemed paid immediately.

**7.1.3 Precedent**

This structure follows precedent from ride-sharing (Uber, Lyft) and delivery platforms (DoorDash, Instacart) that successfully avoid Money Transmitter classification through agency relationships.

### 7.2 Merchant of Record

The Company serves as Merchant of Record for all transactions:

(a) The Company's name appears on End User credit card statements  
(b) The Company is responsible for handling refunds, chargebacks, and disputes  
(c) The Company issues IRS Form 1099-NEC to Payees annually (if earnings ≥$600/year)  
(d) Payees are responsible for their own tax obligations

### 7.3 Payment Processor Flexibility

**7.3.1 Right to Change Processors**

The Company reserves the right to change payment processors (e.g., Stripe, PayPal, direct ACH, cryptocurrency, or other methods) at any time to:

(a) Reduce transaction costs for Payees  
(b) Comply with processor terms of service  
(c) Ensure operational sustainability  
(d) Maintain constitutional alignment

**7.3.2 Notice Requirements**

Any change in payment processor shall be announced at least 30 days in advance, with explanation of reasoning published on the Constitutional Transparency Blog.

**7.3.3 Constitutional Escape Hatch**

If payment processors terminate service due to Open Eppic's constitutional principles (not content violations), the Platform commits to:

(a) Migrate to alternative processors within 90 days  
(b) Explore alternative payment methods (crypto, ACH, alternative processors)  
(c) Communicate transparently with community about options  
(d) Dissolve per Article IX if no alternatives exist that respect our values

The Company will NEVER compromise Constitutional Commitments to retain payment processors. Mission fidelity supersedes operational convenience.

### 7.4 Transaction Fee Absorption

The Company absorbs payment processing fees (typically 2.9% \+ $0.30 per transaction via Stripe). These fees are deducted from Platform's 20% revenue share, NOT from Creator or Element Owner shares.

**Rationale:** Constitutional Commitment \#3 (Ownership & Stewardship) requires protecting creator earnings. Platform bears infrastructure costs.

### 7.5 Tax Reporting

**7.5.1 1099-NEC Issuance**

The Company will issue IRS Form 1099-NEC to Payees who earn $600 or more in a calendar year. Forms delivered electronically by January 31 via payment processor's automated tax service (e.g., Stripe Tax).

**7.5.2 Creator Responsibility**

Payees are solely responsible for:

- Reporting income to IRS and state tax authorities  
- Paying applicable income taxes, self-employment taxes, and sales taxes  
- Consulting tax professionals for guidance  
- Maintaining accurate records

The Platform's role is administrative only (reporting income to IRS via 1099-NEC).

**7.5.3 International Expansion (Future)**

If Platform expands internationally, tax reporting requirements will be determined for each jurisdiction. U.S. tax reporting framework above applies to U.S.-based creators only.

## ARTICLE VIII: INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY AND LICENSING

### 8.1 Element Registration and Licensing

When creators register Elements on the Platform, they:

(a) Retain full ownership of intellectual property  
(b) Grant non-exclusive license to Platform for hosting and display  
(c) Grant licensable rights to other creators per Platform licensing terms  
(d) Commit to minimum availability periods per Element Guidelines

**Platform does NOT claim ownership of user-created Elements.**

### 8.2 Element Availability Commitments

**8.2.1 Cooling-Off Period**

First 14 days after Element registration: Creator may delete freely, no questions asked.

**8.2.2 Minimum Commitment**

After cooling-off period: Elements commit to 1-year minimum availability. This protects derivative creators from having essential elements removed mid-project.

**8.2.3 Renewal Options**

At commitment expiration, Element creator chooses:

(a) **Auto-Renew:** Commitment continues for 1, 2, or 5 years (creator selects)  
(b) **Soft Removal:** New uses blocked, existing derivatives retain perpetual license

**No response at renewal \= Auto-renew** (protects derivative creators from abandonment)

### 8.3 Perpetual Grandfathering

**8.3.1 Core Principle**

Once a Work is published on Open Eppic using validly licensed Elements, that Work receives a perpetual, irrevocable license to use those Elements in that specific Work.

**8.3.2 Element Removal Impact**

If an Element Owner removes their Element from the library (after commitment period expires):

(a) **Existing Works:** All Works published before removal retain perpetual rights to use that Element. These Works remain published and monetized indefinitely.

(b) **New Works:** No new Works may use the removed Element. The Element is no longer available in the library for future derivative works.

(c) **Soft Removal Revenue Choice:** Element Owner may choose to keep earning revenue from existing Works, or forfeit future earnings (earnings redirect to Work creators).

**8.3.3 Constitutional Rationale**

This structure aligns with standard intellectual property licensing practice:

When a music sample license expires, existing albums using that sample remain legal and continue selling. Only new albums are prohibited from using the sample.

Similarly, Open Eppic protects derivative creators' investment in their published Works while respecting Element Owners' right to stop licensing for future use.

**8.3.4 Safety Exception**

Perpetual grandfathering does NOT apply to safety-related removals. If an Element must be removed due to:

- Harassment, stalking, doxxing, or credible safety threats  
- Court order or legal requirement  
- Constitutional policy violation

Then the Platform may remove the Element immediately AND archive dependent Works after giving creators 90 days notice to rework their content. Safety exceptions are rare and require Constitutional Guardian approval within 72 hours of evidence submission.

### 8.4 Public Domain Elements

Platform maintains a curated library of public domain elements (Shakespeare characters, Greek gods, fairy tales, etc.) available to all creators at no cost. To monetize a public domain element derivative, creator must add 200+ word unique description or substantial creative contribution.

### 8.5 Copyright Enforcement

**8.5.1 DMCA Compliance**

Platform complies with Digital Millennium Copyright Act (DMCA):

(a) Designated DMCA agent registered with U.S. Copyright Office  
(b) Content removed within 24 hours of valid DMCA notice  
(c) User notified and may file counter-notice per DMCA procedures  
(d) Repeat infringers (3+ DMCA violations) permanently banned

**8.5.2 Pre-Screening System**

Platform uses AI similarity screening (vector embeddings) to detect potential copyright infringement at Element registration. Flagged elements reviewed by Constitutional Guardian before approval.

**8.5.3 User Reporting**

Users may report suspected copyright infringement. Reports reviewed within 7 business days. Content remains live during review unless DMCA notice filed.

## ARTICLE IX: CONTENT POLICY

### 9.1 Content Standards Authority

Content policy is established in the "Open Eppic Content Guidelines" document, incorporated by reference. Constitutional Guardian enforces policy with oversight by Purpose Trust.

### 9.2 Policy Principles

Content policy adheres to Constitutional Commitment \#6 (Creative Freedom Within Bounds), enforcing PG-13 to R-rated standards comparable to published fiction sold on mainstream platforms.

### 9.3 Prohibited Content (Platform-Wide)

The following content is prohibited for ALL users:

(a) Sexual content involving minors (zero tolerance)  
(b) Explicit sexual acts (genitalia descriptions, penetration, arousal details)  
(c) Sexual assault or rape \- ALL depictions prohibited, including off-screen and implied  
(d) Graphic torture (dwelling on suffering for entertainment)  
(e) Hate speech and dehumanization (denying human dignity of any person)  
(f) Harassment, doxxing, stalking of real individuals  
(g) Instructions for illegal activities

**Rape Ban Rationale:**  
While sexual assault is a serious topic in literature, the maturity level and discomfort this subject creates makes it unsuitable for Open Eppic's collaborative environment. Good stories can address trauma, recovery, and justice without depicting the assault itself.

### 9.4 Allowed Content

Within the bounds above, creators have wide freedom:

(a) Violence within story context (combat, injury, character death) with brief descriptions  
(b) Romance, physical affection, implied sexuality (fade-to-black)  
(c) Dark themes (addiction, trauma, PTSD, abuse) handled respectfully  
(d) Controversial ideas, diverse political viewpoints, social commentary  
(e) Villains committing evil acts (depicting ≠ endorsing)  
(f) Non-sexual profanity (shit, damn, hell, bastard)

### 9.5 Canon Status Not a Moderation Concern

**CRITICAL PRINCIPLE:** Platform moderates for safety and appropriateness, NOT for creative quality or canonical consistency.

Derivative creators may:

- Depict violence, physical harm, or death to elements (within content policy)  
- Write non-canonical stories, alternate universes, "what-if" scenarios  
- Explore element relationships, personality traits, and development  
- Create content Element Owners might dislike (as long as policy-compliant)

**Element Owners have no special content protections beyond platform-wide policy.**

Element Owners who are uncomfortable with derivatives having creative freedom should not register Elements on Open Eppic. Platform policy protects ALL users equally; Element Owners do not receive "canonical control" over derivative works.

### 9.6 Moderation Methodology

**9.6.1 Hybrid Approach**

- Automated flagging (AI keyword detection via Hive.ai or equivalent)  
- Constitutional Guardian review within 24 hours for flagged content  
- Contextual judgment for edge cases  
- Documented precedents published on Transparency Blog

**9.6.2 Standard: "Don't Dwell, Don't Relish, Don't Detail"**

Content that serves the story and is described briefly \= Allowed  
Content that dwells on graphic details for shock value \= Prohibited

**9.6.3 Transparency Commitment**

All moderation decisions explained on Constitutional Transparency Blog. Users may appeal decisions with additional context. Platform commits to good faith efforts, publicly acknowledging when we fall short.

### 9.7 Processor Compliance

Platform reserves right to adjust content policy if required by payment processors, banking partners, or legal compliance. Changes announced 90 days in advance with explanation.

If processors require changes that violate Constitutional Commitments, Platform will migrate to alternative infrastructure per Article VII, Section 7.3.3.

## ARTICLE X: DISSOLUTION

### 10.1 Voluntary Dissolution

Class A Members may vote to dissolve the Company:

(a) Requires 75% supermajority vote of all membership units (Class A \+ Class B)  
(b) Purpose Trust (Golden Share holder) must approve  
(c) If Trust vetoes, dissolution does not occur

### 10.2 Involuntary Dissolution

Company may be dissolved:

(a) By court order  
(b) By Purpose Trust exercise of Call Option followed by dissolution decision  
(c) By operation of law (bankruptcy, regulatory action, etc.)

### 10.3 Constitutional Dissolution

If Company cannot operate while maintaining Constitutional Commitments (e.g., all payment processors refuse service, legal environment makes mission impossible), Class A Members or Purpose Trust may initiate dissolution.

**The Company will dissolve rather than compromise principles.**

### 10.4 Asset Distribution Upon Dissolution

Upon dissolution, assets distributed in following priority:

(a) **First:** Creditors and debts paid in full  
(b) **Second:** Employee wages and benefits (30 days)  
(c) **Third:** Creator/Element Owner balances owed (payment obligations)  
(d) **Fourth:** Remaining assets distributed to Class A Members pro-rata

Purpose Trust (Golden Share) receives $0 upon dissolution (no economic rights).

### 10.5 Open Source Transition

Upon dissolution, the Company commits to:

(a) Open-sourcing platform code (if technically feasible)  
(b) Releasing anonymized financial data for transparency  
(c) Publishing lessons learned and recommendations for future platforms  
(d) Transferring community to alternative platform if one exists that respects similar principles

Mission continues beyond Company's existence.

## APPENDIX A: DEFINITIONS

**Active Users:** Platform users who have logged in at least once in the past 90 days.

**Big IP:** Elements from established franchises with significant commercial value and market recognition. Platform assigns Big IP designation and share weighting (1-30 shares).

**Call Option:** Voluntary grant by Class A Members allowing Purpose Trust to purchase their units at $0.01 per unit upon constitutional violation.

**Class A Members:** Members holding Class A membership units with voting and economic rights.

**Constitutional Commitments:** The nine immutable principles defined in Article II, Section 2.2.

**Constitutional Guardian:** Operational role responsible for daily content moderation and constitutional compliance monitoring. NOT the same as Purpose Trust.

**Constitutional Matters:** Topics subject to Purpose Trust veto authority, defined in Article III, Section 3.2.1.

**Element Pool:** 40% of transaction value distributed to Element Owners whose elements are used in a Work.

**Golden Share:** The single Class B membership unit held by Purpose Trust, granting veto authority over Constitutional Matters but $0 economic value.

**Grandfathering:** Once Work is published, revenue splits and licensing terms are locked indefinitely. Future changes do not apply retroactively.

**Open Eppic Purpose Trust:** Kansas Charitable Trust holding the Golden Share and protecting Constitutional Commitments through stewardship ownership structure.

**Payees:** Creators and Element Owners receiving revenue distributions from Work sales.

**Perpetual License:** Once Work is published with valid Element license, that Work retains irrevocable right to use those Elements indefinitely.

**Purpose Trust:** See "Open Eppic Purpose Trust."

**Small IP:** Independently created elements not part of major franchises. Each Small IP element receives 1 share in Small IP Bucket (10% of transaction value).

**Stewardship Ownership:** Governance model where Purpose Trust holds Golden Share protecting constitutional mission, and Class A Members voluntarily grant Call Option ensuring mission fidelity supersedes financial returns.

**Work:** Complete creative output (story, novel, series) created using Elements and published on Platform.
