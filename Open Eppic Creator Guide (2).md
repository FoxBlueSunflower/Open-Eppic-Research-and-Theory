*Version 2.0 | Last Updated: December 2025*

## Introduction

Welcome to Open Eppic, a collaborative storytelling platform where creators earn revenue from original fiction and derivative works. This guide explains how to get started as a creator, what you can monetize, and how the platform works.

**Key Concepts:**

- **Works**: Complete stories you write and publish  
- **Elements**: Original story components (characters, settings, systems, items, plots) that you or others can reuse  
- **Derivative Works**: Stories that incorporate Elements created by other users  
- **Revenue Sharing**: Fair, transparent splits between creators, Element owners, and platform

---

## Account Types

### Reader

**Purpose**: Read and enjoy content on the platform

**What You Can Do:**

- Browse and read free Works  
- Purchase paid Works  
- Follow favorite creators  
- Leave comments and reviews

**What You Cannot Do:**

- Create or publish Works  
- Register Elements  
- Earn revenue

**Cost**: Free to create account. Pay only for Works you purchase.

---

### Creator

**Purpose**: Write stories, create Elements, and earn revenue

**What You Can Do:**

- Write and publish Works (free or paid)  
- Register original Elements for others to use  
- Use other creators' Elements in your Works  
- Earn revenue from Work sales  
- Earn passive income when others use your Elements  
- Manage multiple pen names/brands (optional)

**Requirements:**

- Must be 18+ years old  
- Must be located in United States  
- Must complete identity verification (Stripe) before receiving payments

**Cost**: Free to create and publish. Platform takes 20% of sales (you keep 80% if using only your own Elements, or 40% if using others' Elements).

## Getting Started as a Creator

### Step 1: Create Your Account

1. Choose username (this becomes your default creator brand)  
2. Provide email and date of birth (18+ required)  
3. Set up profile (optional bio, avatar, social links)

**Note**: Your username is public and appears on everything you create.

### Step 2: Decide What to Create

**Option A: Just Write Works**

- Use only public domain Elements (free)  
- Use other creators' Elements (share revenue with them)  
- No need to register your own Elements

**Option B: Register Elements**

- Create detailed, original story components  
- Allow others to use them in derivative Works  
- Earn passive income when your Elements are used

**Option C: Both**

- Write your own Works  
- Register Elements for passive income  
- Use others' Elements when it enhances your stories

**Most creators do Option C.** There's no rush to decide—you can start with Works and register Elements later.

### Step 3: Understand Revenue

**If You Write a Work Using Only Your Own Elements:**

- You receive: 80% of sale price  
- Platform receives: 20%

**If You Write a Work Using Other Creators' Elements:**

- You receive: 40% of sale price  
- Element owners collectively receive: 40% (split among them)  
- Platform receives: 20%

**If Others Use Your Elements:**

- You receive a share of their 40% Element Pool  
- This is passive income—you earn while others create

**See Monetization Guide for detailed breakdowns and examples.**

## Understanding Elements

### What Is an Element?

An Element is an **original, unique, detailed story component** that can be reused across multiple Works.

**Element Types:**

**Characters**

- Entities that can act, modify, or create  
- Examples: A detective, an AI entity, a sentient dragon

**Settings**

- Locations or environments at any scale  
- Examples: A floating city, a pocket dimension, a haunted library

**Systems**

- Collections of rules defining how something works  
- Examples: A magic system, a government structure, an economic model

**Items**

- Physical or informational objects  
- Examples: A cursed sword, a map to hidden treasure, a forbidden tome

**Plots**

- Highly specific story structures (NOT generic archetypes)  
- Examples: "The Seven Trials of the Crimson Archive" (specific), NOT "The Hero's Journey" (generic)

### Requirements for Monetizable Elements

To register an Element that earns revenue splits, it must be:

1. **Original**: Created by you, not copied from copyrighted works  
2. **Unique**: Distinctive enough to be recognizable  
3. **Detailed**: Minimum 200 words of description  
4. **Defined**: Clear boundaries for how it can be used

**Example of Good Element:**

**Character: Detective Sarah Chen**

Sarah Chen is a 40-year-old private investigator in Neo-Tokyo, 2087\. She has cybernetic implants that enhance her pattern recognition but cause chronic headaches. Former police officer, left the force after refusing to cover up corruption. Drives a vintage 2040 Honda Civic she rebuilt herself. Speaks Mandarin, English, and Japanese. Known for solving impossible cases but terrible at maintaining relationships. Has a cat named Algorithm who she talks to while working through cases. Weaknesses: Trusts too easily, drinks too much coffee, refuses to carry a gun despite dangerous work...

**Example of Bad Element:**

**Character: A Detective**

A detective who solves crimes.

**See Element Guidelines for detailed requirements and examples.**

### What Happens When You Register an Element

**Cooling-Off Period (14 days):**

- You can delete freely, no questions asked  
- Other creators cannot use it yet (prevents abuse)  
- Test whether you're comfortable sharing

**After Cooling-Off (Minimum 1 Year Commitment):**

- Your Element becomes available in library  
- Other creators can use it in their Works  
- You cannot delete until commitment expires  
- You earn revenue splits when used in paid Works

**At Commitment Expiration:**

- **Auto-Renew**: Continue for 1, 2, or 5 more years (you choose)  
- **Soft Removal**: New uses blocked, but existing Works keep using it

**If No Response**: Auto-renews to 1 year (protects derivative creators)

### Perpetual Grandfathering (IMPORTANT)

Once someone publishes a Work using your Element, **that Work keeps the right to use it forever**—even if you later remove your Element from the library.

**What This Means:**

- Removing your Element prevents NEW Works from using it  
- Existing published Works retain permanent license  
- You choose: keep earning from existing Works OR forfeit earnings

**Why This Exists:**

- Protects derivative creators' investment  
- Aligns with standard IP licensing (like music samples)  
- You can stop future licensing without destroying past Works

**Safety Exception**: If Element must be removed for harassment, court order, or policy violation, existing Works get 90 days to rework before archiving.

## Derivative Elements

### What Are Derivative Elements?

Derivative Elements build upon existing original Elements. They exist for **community formation and discovery**, NOT direct monetization.

**Example:**

- Original: "Harry Potter" (Big IP, owned by Warner Bros)  
- Derivative: "Vampire Harry Potter" (your transformative version)

**Key Rule**: Derivative Elements **do NOT receive revenue splits**. Revenue flows to the original Element(s) you declared as sources.

### Why Create Derivative Elements?

**Community Benefits:**

- Works using your derivative cluster together in discovery  
- Creators find your unique interpretation  
- You build reputation as creative transformer  
- Enable fanfic-style communities around popular IP

**Monetization Path (Indirect):**

- You earn CREATOR revenue (40-80%) by writing Works using your derivative  
- You DO NOT earn ELEMENT revenue from your derivative  
- Build community, then monetize through your stories

**Example Revenue Flow:**

You create "Vampire Harry Potter" derivative (based on Harry Potter Big IP).

Someone writes a story using your derivative and sells it for $10.

**Revenue Split:**

- Story creator: $4.00 (40%)  
- Harry Potter (original): $4.00 (40% Element Pool)  
- Platform: $2.00 (20%)  
- **You (derivative creator): $0.00**

But if **you** write a story using your own "Vampire Harry Potter" derivative:

- You (story creator): $4.00 (40%)  
- Harry Potter (original): $4.00 (40% Element Pool)  
- Platform: $2.00 (20%)  
- Your derivative element: $0.00 (derivatives never earn splits)

**You earn as a creator, not as an element owner.**

### Should You Create Derivative or Original?

**Create Derivative If:**

- You want to build on existing popular IP  
- You want community clustering (fanfic communities)  
- You're okay forgoing direct Element revenue

**Create Original If:**

- Your Element is sufficiently unique  
- You want Element revenue splits  
- You want full control over monetization

**Converting Derivative to Original:** If your derivative becomes sufficiently distinct, petition Constitutional Guardian for conversion:

- 500+ words unique description  
- Demonstrated uniqueness from sources  
- Guardian approval required

## Managing Multiple Brands

You can manage **multiple creator identities** (pen names, series, different projects) under one account.

**Why Use Multiple Brands:**

- Separate pen names for different genres  
- Keep completed series distinct from active projects  
- Professional separation (real name vs pseudonym)

**How to Create Additional Brands:**

1. Account Settings → Brand Management → "Add New Brand"  
2. Enter brand name  
3. When registering Elements, assign them to specific brand  
4. Each brand operates independently

**Example:**

- **Brand A**: "Jane Doe \- Fantasy Novels" (your real name, completed trilogy)  
- **Brand B**: "J.D. Mystery" (pen name, ongoing mystery series)  
- **Brand C**: "Jane Doe \- Short Stories" (experimental work)

All brands use same account, but readers see them as separate creators.

## Content Guidelines Summary

Open Eppic allows wide creative freedom within moral and legal boundaries.

### Prohibited Content (Zero Tolerance)

**Platform will remove immediately:**

1. Sexual content involving minors (anyone under 18\)  
2. Explicit sexual acts (detailed descriptions of genitalia, arousal, penetration)  
3. Sexual assault or rape—**ALL depictions** including off-screen, implied, fade-to-black  
4. Graphic torture (dwelling on suffering for entertainment)  
5. Hate speech and dehumanization  
6. Harassment or doxxing of real individuals  
7. Instructions for illegal activities  
8. Copyright infringement

**Consequences**: Account termination, all earnings forfeited

### Allowed Content

**Within the bounds above, you have wide freedom:**

- Violence within story context (combat, injury, death) with brief descriptions  
- Romance, physical affection, implied sexuality (fade-to-black before explicit acts)  
- Dark themes (addiction, trauma, PTSD, abuse) handled respectfully  
- Non-sexual profanity (shit, damn, hell, bastard, ass)  
- All political viewpoints and controversial ideas  
- Religious and philosophical content  
- Villains committing evil acts (depicting ≠ endorsing)

**Guiding Standard**: "Don't dwell. Don't relish. Don't get creative with graphic details."

**See Content Guidelines for detailed policy and examples.**

## Canon Status and Creative Freedom

### Critical Principle: Canon Status Is NOT a Moderation Concern

**The platform moderates for safety and appropriateness, NOT for creative quality or canonical consistency.**

**What This Means:**

**Derivative Creators May:**

- Depict violence, harm, or death to your Elements (within Content Guidelines)  
- Write non-canonical stories, alternate universes, "what-if" scenarios  
- Explore Element relationships, personality, development  
- Make creative choices you might not make  
- Create content you might dislike (as long as policy-compliant)

**Element Owners Do NOT Have:**

- Veto power over derivative story choices  
- Ability to enforce "canonical accuracy"  
- Special protections beyond platform-wide policy  
- Right to remove content because it's "wrong" interpretation

**Element Owners DO Have:**

- Right to report Content Guideline violations  
- Right to remove Elements at commitment expiration (new uses blocked)  
- Right to keep or forfeit earnings from existing Works

### One Policy for All Creators

**There is no "Element Owner tier" with special content protections.**

All creators—Element Owners and derivative creators alike—follow the same Content Guidelines.

**If you're uncomfortable with derivatives having creative freedom (within Content Guidelines), do not register Elements on Open Eppic.**

## External Licensing (Using Elements Outside Platform)

### Taking Elements to Traditional Publishing

If you want to publish work externally (traditional book deal, self-publishing, film adaptation) that incorporates **community-created Elements**, you must obtain external licenses.

**Current Process (Manual Negotiation):**

**Step 1: Identify What You Need**

- Which community Elements do you want to use?  
- How significant is each to your external work?

**Step 2: Contact Creators**

- Reach out via Platform or email  
- Explain your project and how you'll use their Elements  
- Propose terms: one-time fee, revenue share, or both

**Step 3: Negotiate Fairly**

- Suggested range: $100-$1,000 per Element \+ 2-5% revenue share  
- Always include attribution credit  
- Constitutional Commitment: Justice for Vulnerable (don't exploit power imbalance)

**Step 4: Document Agreement**

- Written contract strongly recommended (even simple email acceptable)  
- Specify: what you're licensing, how you'll use it, payment terms, credit  
- Both parties sign and retain copies

**Step 5: Execute**

- Pay agreed compensation  
- Provide attribution in external work  
- Honor terms throughout publication

### Ideas vs. Expression Distinction

**If you're only using general ideas** (themes, concepts, tropes) from derivatives:

- No license required (ideas are not copyrightable)  
- Optional: Provide "Inspired By" attribution (recognition without payment)

**If you're using specific Elements or expression:**

- License required  
- Fair compensation required  
- Legal protection for both parties

**When in doubt, license.** Protects everyone and maintains community trust.

**See Monetization Guide for more details on external licensing.**

## Getting Paid

### Before You Can Receive Payments

You must complete identity verification through Stripe:

**What Stripe Verifies:**

- You are 18+ years old  
- Your identity (government ID)  
- Your tax information (SSN or EIN via W-9)  
- Your bank account or PayPal for payouts

**What Open Eppic Receives:**

- Verification status only (verified/not verified)  
- Connected account ID  
- Payout status

**What Open Eppic Does NOT Receive:**

- Your Social Security Number or Tax ID  
- Your government ID images  
- Your full bank account number  
- Your W-9 form contents

**All sensitive identity information handled securely by Stripe.** Open Eppic never sees your SSN, Tax ID, or government ID.

### Payout Schedule

**Earnings Assessment**: Monthly (calculated at end of each month) **Payout Timing**: NET-30 (payment within 30 days of month-end) **Minimum Threshold**: $25 to receive payout

**If Below $25**: Balance carries forward to next month **Annual Sweep**: Once per year (December), balances ≥$5 paid out even if below $25

**Example Timeline:**

- **January**: Earn $15 → Balance: $15 (below threshold, no payout)  
- **February**: Earn $20 → Balance: $35 (above threshold)  
- **March 30**: Receive $35 payment via Stripe → Balance resets to $0

### Tax Reporting

**IRS Form 1099-NEC:**

- Issued if you earn $600+ in a calendar year  
- Delivered electronically by January 31  
- Stripe handles W-9 collection and 1099 generation automatically

**Your Tax Responsibilities:**

- Report all earnings on your tax return  
- Pay applicable income and self-employment taxes  
- Consult tax professional with questions

**Platform's Role**: Administrative only (reports income to IRS, does not provide tax advice)

## Market Reality & Expectations

### The Honest Truth About Open Eppic's Market Position

**What We Know:**

* Readers pay for quality serialized fiction (Wattpad proves this)  
* Collaborative storytelling has engaged communities (AO3, fanfiction.net)  
* Creators want legal monetization pathways

**What We Don't Know:**

* Will readers pay for derivative fiction (vs. expecting free fanfic)?  
* Will enough derivative creators accept content restrictions?  
* Will ethical framework (compensating original creators) attract audience?

**Open Eppic is experimental.** We're testing whether a market exists for:

* Legal, monetized derivative works  
* Ethical revenue sharing with Element creators  
* Creative freedom within moral boundaries (no explicit sex, no RPF)

### Success Factors (Based on Wattpad Research)

To succeed as a paid creator on Open Eppic:

* **Build audience first**: 50K-100K reads minimum before significant monetization  
* **Expect slower growth**: Paid content grows audience slower than free  
* **Focus on quality**: High engagement (comments, retention) matters more than volume  
* **Popular genres**: Romance, Fantasy, Thriller, Mystery have highest paid readership  
* **Realistic timeline**: 6-12 months to build viable audience

### Why This Might Work Despite Unknowns

**Advantages Over Traditional Fanfiction:**

* Legal permission (no DMCA threat)  
* Monetization with creator compensation  
* Constitutional protections against platform exploitation  
* Quality curation (content guidelines prevent lowest-common-denominator content)

**Advantages Over Original Fiction:**

* Built-in audience (fans of source Elements)  
* Collaborative universe building  
* Element ecosystem reduces worldbuilding overhead

### Setting Realistic Expectations

**First Year Goals:**

* Prove collaborative model works (people actually use Elements)  
* Test whether readers will pay for derivative fiction  
* Build core creator community (100-500 active creators)  
* Validate revenue splits are fair and sustainable

**DO NOT expect:**

* Immediate income replacement  
* Viral growth from day one  
* Traditional fanfiction audience migration (content restrictions may exclude them)  
* Guaranteed success (this is genuinely experimental)

**DO expect:**

* Slow, steady growth if model resonates  
* Iteration and learning (platform will adapt based on data)  
* Constitutional fidelity (principles won't change for profit)  
* Honest transparency about performance

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Do I have to charge for my Works?** A: No. You can make everything free. Free Works still generate Element usage data for discovery.

**Q: Can I change pricing later?** A: Yes. You control pricing for all your Works at any time.

**Q: What if someone uses my Element without permission?** A: Platform tracks Element usage automatically. Element splits calculated automatically once usage detected. Report suspected violations to [support@openeppic.com](mailto:support@openeppic.com).

**Q: Can I use someone's free Element?** A: Yes. They still earn their element split when your Work is monetized (even if Element was free to use). This encourages generous sharing.

**Q: Can I monetize a generic archetype like "The Reluctant Hero"?** A: No. Generic archetypes are not monetizable. But you CAN create an original, detailed character BASED on that archetype and monetize that.

**Q: How much can I earn from Elements?** A: Depends on usage. Element Pool \= 40% of transaction value, split among all Elements used. The more your Elements are used, the more you earn.

**Q: Can I register real people as Elements?** A: Public domain historical figures yes (with transformative description). Living people no (privacy/publicity rights).

**Q: What happens if I'm unreachable for payment?** A: Revenue held for 1 year with email notifications. After 1 year of no contact, funds donated to Project Gutenberg.

**Q: Can I remove my Element early?** A: You cannot during commitment period (1 year minimum after cooling-off). At expiration, choose Auto-Renew or Soft Removal.

**Q: Do existing Works pay me forever?** A: Yes, unless you choose to forfeit earnings at removal. Published Works have perpetual license.

**Q: Can I register Elements from my unpublished work?** A: Yes, but consider waiting until work is complete to avoid complications.

**Q: What if someone registers my Element before me?** A: File DMCA notice if you own copyright. Platform investigates copyright claims within 7 days.

**Q: Can I sell my Element outside Open Eppic?** A: Yes, you retain all rights. Open Eppic license is non-exclusive.
