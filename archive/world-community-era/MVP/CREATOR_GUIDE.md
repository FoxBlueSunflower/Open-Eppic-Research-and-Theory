# **Open Eppic Creator Guide (MVP)**

*Version 1.0 | Last Updated: December 2025*

---

## **Introduction**

Welcome to Open Eppic, a collaborative storytelling platform where creators build on each other's work. This guide explains how to get started as a creator and how the platform works.

**Key Concepts:**

* **Works**: Complete stories you write and publish  
* **Elements**: Original story components (characters, settings, systems, items, plots) that you or others can reuse  
* **Derivative Works**: Stories that incorporate Elements created by other users

---

## **Account Types**

### **Reader**

**Purpose**: Read and enjoy content on the platform

**What You Can Do:**

* Browse and read Works  
* Save works and elements to your library

**What You Cannot Do:**

* Create or publish Works  
* Register Elements

**Cost**: Free

---

### **Creator**

**Purpose**: Write stories and create Elements for collaborative storytelling

**What You Can Do:**

* Write and publish Works  
* Register original Elements for others to use  
* Use other creators' Elements in your Works  
* Manage multiple pen names (optional)

**Requirements:**

* Must be 13+ years old  
* Must be located in United States

**Cost**: Free

---

## **Getting Started as a Creator**

### **Step 1: Create Your Account**

1. Choose username (this becomes your default creator brand)  
2. Provide email and confirm you're 13+  
3. Set up profile (optional bio, avatar, social links)

**Note**: Your username is public and appears on everything you create.

### **Step 2: Decide What to Create**

**Option A: Just Write Works**

* Use only public domain Elements (free)  
* Use other creators' Elements  
* No need to register your own Elements

**Option B: Register Elements**

* Create detailed, original story components  
* Allow others to use them in derivative Works  
* Build recognition in the community

**Option C: Both**

* Write your own Works  
* Register Elements for others to use  
* Use others' Elements when it enhances your stories

**Most creators do Option C.** There's no rush to decide—you can start with Works and register Elements later.

## **Understanding Elements**

### **What Is an Element?**

An Element is an **original, unique, detailed story component** that can be reused across multiple Works.

**Element Types:**

**Characters**

* Entities that can act, modify, or create  
* Examples: A detective, an AI entity, a sentient dragon

**Settings**

* Locations or environments at any scale  
* Examples: A floating city, a pocket dimension, a haunted library

**Systems**

* Collections of rules defining how something works  
* Examples: A magic system, a government structure, an economic model

**Items**

* Physical or informational objects  
* Examples: A cursed sword, a map to hidden treasure, a forbidden tome

**Plots**

* Highly specific story structures (NOT generic archetypes)  
* Examples: "The Seven Trials of the Crimson Archive" (specific), NOT "The Hero's Journey" (generic)

### **Requirements for Elements**

To register an Element, it must be:

1. **Original**: Created by you, not copied from copyrighted works  
2. **Unique**: Distinctive enough to be recognizable  
3. **Detailed**: Minimum 200 words of description  
4. **Defined**: Clear boundaries for how it can be used

**Example of Good Element:**

**Character: Detective Sarah Chen**

Sarah Chen is a 40-year-old private investigator in Neo-Tokyo, 2087\. She has cybernetic implants that enhance her pattern recognition but cause chronic headaches. Former police officer, left the force after refusing to cover up corruption. Drives a vintage 2040 Honda Civic she rebuilt herself. Speaks Mandarin, English, and Japanese. Known for solving impossible cases but terrible at maintaining relationships. Has a cat named Algorithm who she talks to while working through cases. Weaknesses: Trusts too easily, drinks too much coffee, refuses to carry a gun despite dangerous work...

**Example of Bad Element:**

**Character: A Detective**

A detective who solves crimes.

**See Element Guidelines for detailed requirements and examples.**

### **What Happens When You Register an Element**

**After Registration (Minimum 1 Year Commitment):**

* Your Element becomes available in library  
* Other creators can use it in their Works  
* You cannot delete until commitment expires

### **Perpetual License (IMPORTANT)**

Once someone publishes a Work using your Element, **that Work keeps the right to use it forever**—even if you later remove your Element from the library.

**What This Means:**

* Removing your Element prevents NEW Works from using it  
* Existing published Works retain permanent license  
* Protects derivative creators' investment

## **Derivative Elements**

### **What Are Derivative Elements?**

Derivative Elements build upon existing original Elements. They exist for **community formation and discovery**.

**Example:**

**Original Element**: "Detective Sarah Chen" (Neo-Tokyo investigator with cybernetic implants)

**Derivative Element**: "Vampire Detective Sarah Chen" (transformation: now a vampire investigating supernatural crimes)

### **Why Create Derivative Elements?**

* Works using your derivative cluster together  
* Creators discover your unique interpretation  
* Build reputation as creative transformer  
* Create Works using your derivative interpretation

### **Requirements**

All derivative Elements must:

1. Identify original sources (no nesting derivatives)  
2. Write 200+ unique words describing transformation  
3. Acknowledge connection to original sources

**Derivative Elements do NOT compete with originals for recognition—they exist to build communities around specific interpretations.**

---

## **Canon vs. Non-Canon**

### **Critical Understanding**

**CANON STATUS IS NOT A MODERATION CONCERN**

You can write whatever interpretation you want within Content Guidelines. Element Owners do NOT have:

* Veto power over derivative story choices  
* Ability to enforce "canonical accuracy"  
* Right to remove content for being "wrong interpretation"

**One policy for all creators.** If Element Owner is uncomfortable with derivatives having creative freedom within Content Guidelines, they should not register Elements on Open Eppic.

### **Derivative Elements Signal Intent**

If you want to write canonically-aligned stories, use the **original Element**.

If you want to explore alternative interpretations, create a **derivative Element** and use that.

**Example:**

**Canonical Work**: Uses "Detective Sarah Chen" (original)

**Non-Canonical Work**: Uses "Vampire Detective Sarah Chen" (derivative)

Both are allowed. Both follow Content Guidelines. Neither is "better"—just different community clusters.

## **Content Guidelines Overview**

Open Eppic maintains content standards comparable to published fiction (PG-13 to R-rated).

### **Strictly Prohibited**

* Graphic sexual content  
* Sexual content involving minors (any form)  
* Graphic violence (detailed gore, torture)  
* Hate speech and dehumanization  
* Sexual assault/rape (all depictions)  
* Animal cruelty (extensive/gratuitous)  
* Harassment and doxxing

### **Allowed (with content warnings)**

* Violence within story context (non-graphic)  
* Adult themes (implied, "fade to black")  
* Political content (all viewpoints)  
* Difficult moral questions  
* Mature themes handled responsibly

**Moderation Philosophy**: "I disagree" or "This offends me" ≠ grounds for removal. We moderate for safety, not taste.

**See Content Guidelines for complete policy.**

## **Using Public Domain Content**

You can freely use public domain works:

* Classic literature (Shakespeare, Austen, etc.)  
* Mythology (Greek, Norse, Egyptian, etc.)  
* Historical figures (deceased)  
* Fairy tales and folklore

**Platform maintains curated library** of verified public domain Elements.

**You CAN register**: Detailed transformative versions (200+ unique words) **You CANNOT register**: Generic public domain Elements as-is

## **Frequently Asked Questions**

**Q: Can I use someone's Element without permission?** A: Yes, once it's registered on the platform. That's the whole point—Elements are shared for collaborative storytelling.

**Q: What if someone uses my Element in a way I don't like?** A: If it violates Content Guidelines, report it. If it's just a creative choice you disagree with, that's allowed. Canon status is not a moderation concern.

**Q: Can I remove my Element early?** A: No, not during commitment period (1 year minimum). At expiration, choose Auto-Renew or Soft Removal.

**Q: Do existing Works keep using my Element forever?** A: Yes. Published Works have perpetual license.

**Q: Can I register Elements from my unpublished work?** A: Yes, but consider waiting until work is complete to avoid complications.

**Q: What if someone registers my Element before me?** A: File DMCA notice if you own copyright.

**Q: Can I use my Element outside Open Eppic?** A: Yes, you retain all rights. Open Eppic license is non-exclusive.

**Q: Can I register real people as Elements?** A: You may request Open Eppic add a public domain historical figure. Living people, no (privacy/publicity rights).

**Q: What if I'm uncomfortable with derivatives?** A: Don't register Elements. Use them only in your own Works, or wait until you're more comfortable with collaborative storytelling.
