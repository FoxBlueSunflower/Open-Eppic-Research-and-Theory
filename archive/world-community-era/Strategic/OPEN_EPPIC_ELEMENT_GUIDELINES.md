# OPEN EPPIC ELEMENT GUIDELINES

**Version:** 2.0  
**Last Updated:** December 2025

## INTRODUCTION

Elements are the building blocks of collaborative storytelling on Open Eppic. They allow creators to share original story components that others can incorporate into their Works while earning passive revenue.

This guide explains:

- What makes an Element monetizable  
- How to create high-quality Elements  
- Element commitment periods and removal  
- Best practices for maximizing your Element's value

---

## WHAT IS AN ELEMENT?

An Element is an **original, unique, detailed story component** that can be reused by other creators.

### Element Types

**CHARACTERS**: Entities that can act, modify, or create

- Humans, aliens, gods, personified animals, AI entities  
- From cosmic beings to talking mice  
- Must have agency (ability to make choices/take action)

**SETTINGS**: Locations or environments (any scale)

- Planets, dimensions, cities, buildings, rooms  
- From infinite cosmic spaces to microscopic dimensions  
- Physical or metaphysical locations

**SYSTEMS**: Collections of rules defining how something works

- Magic systems, governance systems, economic systems  
- Dimensional mechanics, laws of physics  
- Social structures, technological frameworks

**ITEMS**: Physical or informational objects

- Weapons, artifacts, tools, documents  
- Directed by characters, may be governed by systems  
- Cannot act independently (that's a character)

**PLOTS**: Detailed story structures (only if highly specific)

- Must be uniquely yours, not generic archetypes  
- "The Hero's Journey" \= not monetizable (generic)  
- "The Seven Trials of the Crimson Archive" \= monetizable (specific)

## MONETIZABLE ELEMENT REQUIREMENTS

To register as a monetizable Element, your Element must meet all four requirements:

### 1\. ORIGINAL

Your Element must be created by you and not copied from existing copyrighted works.

**Allowed:**

- Original characters, settings, systems you invented  
- Transformative versions of public domain characters (with 200+ word unique description)  
- Your own creative concepts

**Not Allowed:**

- Direct copies of copyrighted characters (Harry Potter, Darth Vader, etc.)  
- Minor modifications to existing copyrighted works  
- Elements from others' copyrighted franchises without permission

### 2\. UNIQUE

Your Element must be distinctive enough to be recognizable and distinguishable from generic concepts.

**Allowed:**

- "Detective Sarah Chen" \- specific character with defined traits  
- "The Crimson Archive" \- specific magical library with unique properties  
- "The Threefold Path" \- your specific magic system with rules

**Not Allowed:**

- "A Detective" \- too generic  
- "A Magical Library" \- generic concept  
- "Magic" \- generic archetype

**Test**: Can someone describe your Element specifically, or just say "it's like every other \[type\]"?

### 3\. DETAILED

Your Element must be sufficiently developed to be useful in derivative works.

**Minimum**: 200 words of unique description

**What to Include:**

**For Characters:**

- Physical description  
- Personality traits and flaws  
- Background and history  
- Abilities and limitations  
- Motivations and goals  
- Key relationships

**For Settings:**

- Physical layout and scale  
- Atmosphere and mood  
- History and significance  
- Inhabitants or users  
- Key locations within  
- Sensory details (sights, sounds, smells)

**For Systems:**

- Core rules and mechanics  
- Limitations and requirements  
- Exceptions and edge cases  
- Consequences of use  
- Applications and examples  
- How it interacts with other elements

**For Items:**

- Physical properties and appearance  
- Powers, functions, or significance  
- Limitations and requirements  
- History and origin  
- How it's used or activated  
- Current location or owner

### 4\. DEFINED

Your Element must have clear boundaries so derivative creators understand how to use it appropriately.

**Clarify:**

- What the Element can and cannot do  
- Where and when the Element exists or operates  
- Any restrictions or limitations  
- How it interacts with other story components  
- Whether it can be destroyed, modified, or relocated

## MAJOR VS. MINOR ELEMENTS

### Major Elements

Major Elements meet one or more criteria:

**Named in original work:**

- Element has proper name used consistently  
- Not "the old wizard" but "Gandalf the Grey"

**Multiple appearances:**

- Appears in multiple scenes, chapters, or installments  
- Has significant presence across work

**Plot significance:**

- Drives plot significantly  
- Has notable character arc or development  
- Consequences would alter story fundamentally

**Marketed or merchandised:**

- Featured in official merchandise (action figures, posters, art)  
- Used in marketing materials

**Examples**: Harry Potter (character), Hogwarts (setting), The One Ring (item), The Force (system)

### Minor Elements

Minor Elements are:

**Background or incidental:**

- Mentioned briefly or appear in background  
- Minimal impact if removed

**Unnamed:**

- Generic descriptions without proper names  
- "The bartender," "the village," "a sword"

**Limited plot function:**

- No significant impact on story progression  
- Atmospheric or world-building only

**Not merchandised:**

- Not featured in official products

**Examples**: Random Hogwarts student \#47, unnamed tavern, background magic item

### Special Interest Tag

Element creators may tag minor Elements as "Special Interest" to:

- Keep libraries manageable and searchable  
- Allow filtering (users can toggle "Show minor elements" on/off)  
- Signal interesting but non-central Elements

**Note**: Users may appeal if they believe Element is miscategorized.

## PUBLIC DOMAIN ELEMENTS

Public domain content (copyright expired or never copyrighted) may be freely used.

**Platform Maintains Public Domain Library:**

- Curated collection (Shakespeare, Greek gods, fairy tales, historical figures)  
- Verified public domain in US  
- Available to all creators at no cost

**You CANNOT Register:**

- Generic public domain Elements as-is (e.g., "King Arthur" unchanged)

**You CAN Register:**

- Detailed, transformative versions with 200+ word unique description  
- Your specific interpretation, setting, or modifications  
- Example: "King Arthur in the Cyber-Realm" with substantial originality

## ELEMENT QUALITY STANDARDS

High-quality Elements are more likely to be used, generating passive income.

### Clarity

- Write clear, concise descriptions anyone can understand  
- Avoid excessive jargon or unnecessarily complex language  
- Define any specialized terms you use  
- Use active voice and concrete details

### Completeness

- Include all essential information derivative creators need  
- Cover appearance, abilities, limitations, history, relationships  
- Anticipate questions users might have  
- Provide examples of how Element could be used

### Consistency

- Ensure internal consistency (no contradictions)  
- If Element is part of series, maintain consistency across Elements  
- Update Element if you make significant changes in your own Works

### Flexibility

- Leave room for derivative creators to interpret and adapt  
- Avoid overly restrictive usage guidelines  
- Focus on core identity rather than every possible scenario  
- Trust derivative creators to respect your Element's essence

## ELEMENT COMMITMENT PERIODS

### Cooling-Off Period

**First 14 days after registration:**

- Delete freely, no questions asked  
- No derivatives can use Element yet (protects against abuse)  
- Test whether you're comfortable with Element being public

### Minimum Commitment

**After cooling-off period:**

- Elements commit to 1-year minimum availability  
- Protects derivative creators from having essential Elements removed mid-project  
- Cannot delete until commitment expires

### Renewal Options

**At commitment expiration**, you choose:

**1\. Auto-Renew (Default)**

- Continue for 1, 2, or 5 years (you select duration)  
- Longer periods reduce renewal email frequency  
- Signals stability to derivative creators

**2\. Soft Removal**

- New uses blocked (Element removed from library)  
- Existing Works retain perpetual license (stay published)  
- You choose: keep earning from existing Works OR forfeit future earnings

**No Response \= Auto-Renew**

- Protects derivative creators from abandonment  
- 90 days advance notice, then 30 days reminder  
- If no choice made, auto-renews for 1 year

## PERPETUAL GRANDFATHERING

**CRITICAL PRINCIPLE:**

Once a Work is published on Open Eppic using your Element, that Work receives a **perpetual, irrevocable license** to use your Element in that specific Work.

### What This Means

**If You Remove Your Element:**

**Existing Works (published before removal):**

- Retain permanent rights to use your Element  
- Remain published and monetized indefinitely  
- Continue generating revenue for you (unless you forfeit)

**New Works (after removal):**

- Cannot use your Element  
- Element no longer available in library  
- No new derivatives possible

### Your Revenue Choice at Removal

**Option A: Keep Earning**

- Existing Works continue paying you Element revenue  
- Passive income from past licensing  
- No action required

**Option B: Forfeit Earnings**

- Stop receiving revenue from existing Works  
- Earnings redirect to Work creators  
- Cannot be undone

### Why Perpetual Grandfathering?

This aligns with standard intellectual property licensing practice:

**Music Sample Analogy:** When a music sample license expires, existing albums using that sample remain legal and continue selling. Only new albums cannot use the sample.

Similarly, we protect derivative creators' investment in their published Works while respecting your right to stop future licensing.

### Safety Exception

Perpetual grandfathering does NOT apply if Element must be removed due to:

**Emergency Safety Removal:**

- Harassment, stalking, doxxing, credible safety threats  
- Court order or legal requirement  
- Constitutional policy violation (hate speech, graphic content, minor sexualization)

**In These Cases:**

- Element removed immediately  
- Derivative creators get 90 days notice to rework content  
- After 90 days, dependent Works archived (not deleted)  
- Requires Constitutional Guardian approval within 72 hours

**Safety exceptions are rare** and require evidence of genuine threat.

## ELEMENT USAGE AND CANON STATUS

### Canon Status is NOT a Moderation Concern

**CRITICAL PRINCIPLE:**

Platform moderates for safety and appropriateness, NOT for creative quality or canonical accuracy.

**Derivative creators may:**

- Depict violence, physical harm, or death to your Element (within Content Guidelines)  
- Write non-canonical stories, alternate universes, "what-if" scenarios  
- Explore your Element's relationships, personality, and development  
- Make creative choices you might not make (within Content Guidelines)  
- Create content you might dislike (as long as policy-compliant)

**Derivative creators may NOT:**

- Violate Content Guidelines (graphic content, hate speech, etc.)  
- Claim ownership of your Element  
- Use your Element outside Platform without separate agreement

### No Element Owner Exclusive Protections

All creators follow the same Content Guidelines. There is no "Element Owner tier" with special protections.

**You do NOT have veto power over:**

- How derivatives depict your Element (within Content Guidelines)  
- Character development or story choices  
- Romantic relationships or character interactions  
- Alternate universe interpretations

**You DO have:**

- Right to remove Element at commitment expiration (new uses blocked)  
- Right to report Content Guideline violations  
- Right to keep or forfeit earnings from existing Works

### If You're Uncomfortable

**If you're uncomfortable with derivatives having creative freedom within Content Guidelines, do not register Elements on Open Eppic.**

Platform policy protects ALL users equally. Element Owners do not receive canonical control over derivative works.

## DERIVATIVE ELEMENTS

### What Are Derivative Elements?

Derivative Elements build upon existing original Elements. They exist for community formation and discovery.

**Key Distinction:**

- **Original Elements**: Monetizable (receive revenue splits)  
- **Derivative Elements**: Not monetizable (receive $0 revenue)

**Examples:**

- "Vampire Harry Potter" (based on Harry Potter)  
- "Detective in Middle-earth" (based on LOTR setting)  
- "Cyberpunk Magic System" (based on existing magic system)

### Why Create Derivative Elements?

**Community Benefits:**

- Works using your derivative cluster together  
- Creators discover your unique interpretation  
- You build reputation as creative transformer

**Monetization Through Works (Not Elements):**

- You cannot earn Element revenue from derivatives  
- BUT you earn Creator revenue (40-80%) by writing Works using your derivative  
- Original Element owners still receive Element revenue

### How to Create Derivative Elements

**Step 1: Identify Original Sources**

- Declare which original Elements your derivative is based on  
- Can declare multiple originals (mix of Small IP and Big IP)  
- Cannot declare other derivatives as sources (no nesting)

**Step 2: Write Unique Description (200+ words)**

- How does it transform the original?  
- What new qualities does it have?  
- How should creators use it?

**Step 3: Register & Acknowledge**

- Platform shows original sources and revenue implications  
- You acknowledge: "Revenue flows to original sources, not to me"  
- Platform validates: No nesting, sufficient uniqueness

### Revenue Example

**Work uses "Vampire Harry Potter" derivative → Work sells $10**

Revenue split:

- Creator: $4.00 (40%)  
- Harry Potter (Big IP, 30 shares): $4.00 (40% Element pool)  
- Platform: $2.00 (20%)  
- "Vampire Harry Potter" creator: $0.00 (derivative not monetizable)

### Converting Derivative to Original

If your derivative becomes sufficiently distinct, you may petition Platform to convert to original Element status.

**Requirements:**

- 500+ words unique description  
- Demonstrated significant uniqueness from sources  
- Constitutional Guardian approval

## ELEMENT DESCRIPTIONS

### Platform-Generated vs. Owner-Provided

**Platform-Generated Description:**

- Extracted from your original work when Element registered  
- Immutable (cannot be changed)  
- Serves as canonical reference for derivatives

**Owner-Provided Description:**

- Your explanation, notes, usage guidelines  
- Can be freely updated/edited anytime  
- Helps derivative creators understand your vision

**Both are visible to users**. Platform-generated ensures stable reference; owner-provided allows flexibility.

## ELEMENT REMOVAL PROCESS

### At Commitment Expiration

**90 days before expiration:**

- Email with renewal options (Auto-Renew 1/2/5 years, Soft Removal)  
- Platform dashboard shows expiration date

**30 days before expiration (if no response):**

- Reminder email

**On expiration date (if no response):**

- Auto-renews to 1-year to protect derivative creators

**If you choose Soft Removal:**

- Element removed from library (new uses blocked)  
- Existing Works keep perpetual license  
- You choose: keep earning OR forfeit earnings

### Safety Emergency Removal

If Element must be removed for safety:

**Submit Evidence:**

- Email [guardians@openeppic.com](mailto:guardians@openeppic.com)  
- Provide evidence of harassment, stalking, doxxing, or credible threat  
- Include screenshots, timestamps, specific concerns

**Guardian Reviews (within 72 hours):**

- Evaluates evidence  
- Determines if safety exception warranted  
- Immediate removal if approved

**Impact on Derivatives:**

- Derivative creators get 90 days notice to rework  
- After 90 days, dependent Works archived  
- Derivative creators can restore by removing dependency

**This exception is RARE** and requires genuine safety threat, not creative disagreement.

## BEST PRACTICES

### Before Registering

**Ask Yourself:**

- Am I comfortable with others interpreting my Element?  
- Am I okay with derivative creators having creative freedom (within Content Guidelines)?  
- Will I keep this Element available for at least 1 year?  
- Is my Element sufficiently developed (200+ words)?

**If uncertain about any**, wait before registering.

### Writing Element Descriptions

**Do:**

- Focus on core identity and defining traits  
- Provide concrete examples and sensory details  
- Include limitations and weaknesses (makes Element more interesting)  
- Write in clear, accessible language  
- Leave room for interpretation

**Don't:**

- Try to control every possible use case  
- Write overly restrictive usage guidelines  
- Assume derivative creators know your unstated assumptions  
- Make your description a full novel (200-500 words is usually sufficient)

### Managing Derivatives

**Do:**

- Trust Content Guidelines to protect your Element  
- Report genuine policy violations (hate speech, graphic content)  
- Engage positively with community if you choose  
- Update your owner-provided description with helpful clarifications

**Don't:**

- Try to enforce "canonical accuracy" (not a moderation concern)  
- Report derivatives just because you dislike creative choices  
- Expect veto power over derivative story decisions  
- Remove Elements rashly (1-year minimum commitment)

### Revenue Optimization

**To maximize Element revenue:**

- Create high-quality, well-described Elements  
- Focus on broadly usable Elements (more potential users)  
- Register Elements from popular existing works (if you own IP)  
- Be known as creator-friendly (encourages use)  
- Keep Elements available long-term (builds ecosystem)

## FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

**Q: Can I change my Element description after registration?** A: You can freely update your owner-provided description. Platform-generated description (from original work) is immutable.

**Q: What if someone misuses my Element?** A: Report Content Guideline violations to [guardians@openeppic.com](mailto:guardians@openeppic.com). Platform will review within 24 hours.

**Q: Can I require derivatives to be "canonical"?** A: No. Canon status is not a moderation concern. Derivatives have creative freedom within Content Guidelines.

**Q: What if I want to remove my Element early?** A: You cannot during commitment period (1 year minimum after cooling-off). At expiration, choose Soft Removal.

**Q: Do existing Works pay me forever?** A: Yes, unless you choose to forfeit earnings at removal. Published Works have perpetual license.

**Q: Can I register Elements from my unpublished work?** A: Yes, but consider waiting until work is complete to avoid complications.

**Q: What if someone registers my Element before me?** A: File DMCA notice if you own copyright. Platform investigates copyright claims within 7 days.

**Q: Can I sell my Element outside Open Eppic?** A: Yes, you retain all rights. Open Eppic license is non-exclusive.

**Q: How much can I earn from Elements?** A: Depends on usage. Element Pool \= 40% of transaction value, split among all Elements used.

**Q: Can I register real people as Elements?** A: Public domain historical figures yes (with transformative description). Living people no (privacy/publicity rights).
